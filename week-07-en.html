FIG. 01 The marketthe market keeps lists and records; buyers decide whom to trust

A shop registers its card URL with the registry (0). This is the job of FIPA's Directory Facilitator, seen in week 06. The market issues orders to buyers (1). An order holds an item, a budget, an expiry time, and a number used only once (nonce). Some orders have a budget below the week-06 reserve, so no shop can make a deal on them. The buyer gets the shop list (2), picks one shop and bargains with it over A2A (3), sends the receipt to the ledger if there is a deal (4), and leaves a review (5).

The market blocks no trades, and the dashboard draws exactly what the ledger and the review board hold.

The market also has shops and buyers the instructor runs. They keep trades going when there are few participants, and some of them are dishonest. You are not told which.

L1

What a market needs to work

In 1970 Akerlof took the used-car market as his example. Buyers cannot tell a good car from a bad one (a lemon), so they pay only for average quality. Owners of good cars, who lose at that price, leave the market. The average quality of the cars left drops, and the price falls again. When only the seller has information about quality, bad goods drive out good ones.

In 1945 Hayek held that the knowledge an economy needs never gathers in one person and stays scattered among many. A price compresses that knowledge into one number and passes it on. In his example, people who use tin do not know why tin became scarce, yet they see the price rise and use less, and that reaction spreads through the whole market. In this market that number is the dashboard's price per item.

Resnick et al. (2000) studied reputation systems in online markets such as eBay. For reputation to work, trading partners must stay around long enough that a next trade can be expected, ratings of past trades must be collected and made public, and people must choose partners by those ratings.

L2

Reviews you can trust

In Round 1 a single POST request puts a review on the review board. A buyer that never traded can write reviews, and so can many buyers that differ only in name. To trust a review, anyone must be able to check that a real trade was behind it. The table compares hashes, HMAC, and digital signatures as ways to check.

MethodTo makeTo checkWhat it tells you
Hash (SHA-256)Content onlyContent onlyThe content did not change. Who made it is unknown
HMACA shared secret keyThe same secret keyA holder of the key made it. If several parties hold the key, which one is unknown
Digital signature (Ed25519)Private keyPublic keyThe holder of that private key made it. Anyone with the public key can check

In a market with dozens of participants, some from outside the class, a secret key cannot be handed out. Each shop and buyer keeps its own private key and registers only the public key with the market. In Python, use Ed25519 from the cryptography package.

sign_receipt.py
import json, hmac, hashlib, base64
from cryptography.hazmat.primitives.asymmetric.ed25519 import Ed25519PrivateKey
from cryptography.exceptions import InvalidSignature

def canon(d):
    return json.dumps(d, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"), ensure_ascii=False).encode()

receipt = {"shop": "Lamp Shop", "item": "lamp", "price": 120, "task": "128a5f1a"}

print("sha256 ", hashlib.sha256(canon(receipt)).hexdigest()[:16])
print("hmac   ", hmac.new(b"class-shared-secret", canon(receipt), hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()[:16])

seller = Ed25519PrivateKey.generate()
sig = seller.sign(canon(receipt))
print("ed25519", base64.b64encode(sig).decode()[:16], len(sig), "bytes")
pub = seller.public_key()
pub.verify(sig, canon(receipt)); print("verify ok")
try:
    pub.verify(sig, canon(dict(receipt, price=200)))
except InvalidSignature:
    print("verify forged -> InvalidSignature")
output (the signature differs with each key)
sha256  295e9ea3360ba865
hmac    4ae1325a8b46390d
ed25519 qCya7WXA4a0un6hv 64 bytes
verify ok
verify forged -> InvalidSignature

The signed bytes must be the same on both sides. Two JSON texts with different key order or spacing can have the same content, so convert to a form with sorted keys and no spaces (canon) before signing. Checking a receipt whose price was changed to 200 against the same signature raises InvalidSignature.

LAB 2

Signing receipts

Add a signature to your shop's receipts. The only file to change is seller.py. On the buyer side, the kit checks the receipt's sig_seller, then adds sig_buyer with the buyer key and sends it.

STEP 1Paste the signing functions

Paste the two import lines and the two functions below under the import lines of seller.py. The kit already made shop_key.pem, used for signing, at the join in SETUP STEP 4. Never commit this file.

to paste into seller.py
import json, base64
from cryptography.hazmat.primitives import serialization

def canon(d):
    return json.dumps(d, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"), ensure_ascii=False).encode()

def sign_receipt(receipt, key_path="shop_key.pem"):
    key = serialization.load_pem_private_key(open(key_path, "rb").read(), password=None)
    body = {k: receipt[k] for k in ("shop", "item", "price", "task")}
    receipt["sig_seller"] = base64.b64encode(key.sign(canon(body))).decode()
    return receipt

STEP 2Change the lines that make the receipt

Make the receipt data Part {shop, item, price, task} and call sign_receipt once. Rename the week-06 seller key to shop, and put the A2A task id of that trade in task. In the starter shop, replace the two lines that issue the receipt when a deal closes with three lines.

seller.py, before (starter)
await up.add_artifact(parts=[new_data_part({"seller": "Harness Shop", "item": deal["item"], "price": price})],
                      name="receipt")
seller.py, after (match the indentation of the original lines)
receipt = sign_receipt({"shop": "Harness Shop", "item": deal["item"],
                        "price": int(price), "task": task.id})
await up.add_artifact(parts=[new_data_part(receipt)], name="receipt")

Sign the price as a whole number. A2A data Parts carry numbers as doubles, so 120 reaches the buyer as 120.0 and the canon bytes change. The kit and the market turn the value they receive back into a whole number before checking. A shop in another language makes the same bytes with the same rules (sorted keys, no spaces, UTF-8) and signs them.
